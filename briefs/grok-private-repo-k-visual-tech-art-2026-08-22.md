# Grok Research Brief: Private-repo-k Visual and Technical-Art Territories

Research three genuinely distinct AAA-ambitious but AA-indie-producible visual and technical-art
territories for a standalone 3D tower-defense survival shooter. This is decision evidence, not an
art-direction selection, asset shopping list, or permission to copy references.

## Product and proof constraints

- Dense combat must keep roughly 20–60 mixed enemies, the player, projectiles, shotgun knockback,
  turret targeting, resources, workbench state, danger, and base damage readable.
- Impact comes from coordinated animation, material response, lighting, camera, audio intent, and
  layered particle/VFX timing; raw particle count is not quality.
- Required VFX situations include muzzle flash, pellet/tracer communication, impact, blood or
  material response, distance/mass-sensitive knockback, turret telegraphs, upgrades, enemy death,
  resource feedback, and base-state escalation.
- Visual and motion claims require named reference frames or clips, reproducible own captures,
  performance evidence, a fresh critic, and an owner taste gate.
- Godot 4 is currently only a leading engine candidate; Unity 6 and Unreal Engine 5 remain credible
  alternatives. Do not choose among them or assume any engine already passed the visual bar.

## Research task

Use current primary sources wherever possible: official art/VFX/animation/rendering documentation,
developer presentations and breakdowns, licensed asset-store terms, engine repositories, and
publicly inspectable reference media. Name exact gameplay situations, not only game titles.

For each territory analyze:

1. Silhouette, value grouping, color hierarchy, materials, lighting, environment composition,
   animation language, camera behavior, UI/world-space signaling, and negative-space strategy.
2. How enemy mass classes, knockback distance, turret targets, projectile paths, resources,
   workbench availability, and base danger remain legible during maximum intended density.
3. A frame-by-frame VFX layering and timing model for the required situations, including what each
   layer communicates and which layers must disappear first under budget pressure.
4. A realistic AA-indie production pipeline for characters, environments, animation, materials,
   shaders, lighting, particles, decals, destruction, LOD/instancing, and iteration.
5. Concrete feasibility and risk in Godot 4, Unity 6, and Unreal Engine 5 without turning this into
   an engine-selection vote.
6. Asset provenance, modification rights, attribution, seat/tool costs, generative-content risks,
   and a ledger structure that keeps licenses recoverable.
7. Performance risks under a named horde and VFX scenario, with measurable frame-time, draw-call,
   overdraw, particle, light, material, memory, and loading budgets. Numbers are proposals until
   measured and owner-promoted.

## Required deliverables

1. Exactly three clearly separable visual/technical-art territories.
2. At least four named reference products and twelve exact frames, clips, or gameplay situations
   across the full answer, each with a public URL and a precise lesson.
3. For each territory: positive anchors, negative examples, production path, staffing/skill
   burden, engine-specific feasibility, risks, and explicit reasons it may fail the product.
4. A common readability rubric written before any game frame exists, covering horde hierarchy,
   player focus, knockback/mass, turret intent, resource/workbench state, base danger, and impact.
5. A compact capture plan: fixed replay, named camera states, stills, fixed-tick frame sequences,
   bounded clips, structured runtime state, and performance metrics. Never use a green build as
   visual proof.
6. A VFX budget and degradation ladder that preserves gameplay information before spectacle.
7. A provenance/license evidence schema and explicit external-cost boundaries.
8. The smallest owner taste decision the resulting evidence can support; do not make it.

Separate primary-source fact, visual inference, production estimate, proposal, and `unknown`.
Attack the premise that maximum realism, maximum particles, or the engine with the highest visual
ceiling automatically produces the strongest or most achievable art direction.
