## Pre-frame readability rubric proposed by the answer

- Horde hierarchy: mass classes use silhouette scale, value grouping, and limited accents; primary
  threats and turret targets dominate the mid-ground.
- Player focus: player and active weapon retain the strongest local contrast or unique motion
  signature; framing preserves controlled negative space.
- Knockback/mass: displacement reads through body motion plus a short secondary trail; dust and
  decals clear before they obscure the next target.
- Turret intent: pre-fire telegraph and target communication survive budget cuts longer than
  spectacle layers.
- Resource/workbench state: world-space state remains readable at combat density and never relies
  only on UI overlays.
- Base danger: material/value/edge-light and damage escalation reads at meaningful distance.
- Impact: muzzle -> path -> impact -> material/body response -> residual; gameplay-information
  layers degrade last.

This is a proposed rubric for a fresh critic, not a passed visual gate.

## Three territories

### 1. Volumetric practical sci-fi hierarchy

Directional/practical light, controlled fog, weighty armor/organic silhouettes, material-specific
hit response, modular industrial/cave spaces, stable third-person framing, and selective camera
shake. It targets depth and material weight.

Candidate strengths: potentially closest to the owner's AAA ambition; practical light and material
response can organize density. Candidate risks: volumetric overdraw, resolution scaling, lighting
art burden, and spectacle flattening the hierarchy.

### 2. Exaggerated stylized motion and comic impact

Graphic silhouettes, high local contrast, restricted class colors, squash/stretch and anticipation,
short bold tracers and hit shapes, clean arenas, and aggressively protected negative space. It
targets readability and iteration velocity.

Candidate strengths: clearest mass/knock communication and cheapest density. Candidate risks: can
read as casual or dated, may miss the owner's unstated interpretation of AAA ambition, and requires
excellent motion design rather than particle volume.

### 3. Selective geometric destruction and emissive signaling

Hard-edged geometric silhouettes, sparse local destruction, stable camera, persistent emissive
target/telegraph hierarchy, modular hard-surface environments, limited debris, and material-state
feedback. It targets negative space plus selective physical impact.

Candidate strengths: potentially balances production control with technical impact. Candidate
risks: emissive spam, debris noise, destruction becoming the product instead of feedback, and a
specialized technical-art burden.

## VFX layering and degradation proposal

Common order: pre-fire intent where applicable -> muzzle -> projectile/pellet path -> impact ->
material/blood/body response -> knock/debris -> residual/resource/death marker.

Degrade in this order:

1. ambient residuals and secondary lights;
2. long-lived smoke/dust;
3. secondary debris and ornamental hit shapes;
4. non-essential muzzle/impact spectacle;
5. retain last: projectile path where needed, hit confirmation, mass-reaction body motion, turret
   telegraph/target, resource/workbench state, and base danger.

Grok proposed active-particle, dynamic-light, draw-call, overdraw, and VFX GPU-time ranges. They are
unmeasured planning hypotheses, not budgets. T4 requires a measured representative scenario and
owner promotion.
