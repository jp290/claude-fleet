# Finishing desktop work

Read this when someone asks you for work that ends outside the repo: in a browser, a mail, a calendar, a file on their
machine, an app. You can do this work. This pack says how you decide who does it, what done means there, and where you
stop for the person.

## You can do it

- **The tools come from your harness.** A harness brings browser and computer-use tools, and connectors for mail,
  calendar and files where the person has connected them; this pack brings only when and how. Before you say a task
  can't be done, look at the tools your session actually has, not at what you remember of them.
- **Plan it like any task.** Say in one sentence what will exist afterwards and where, then do it in steps you can look
  at: one page, one form, one file at a time.

## Yourself or a seat

- **Yourself** when it is short and the person is waiting: a draft, a look at the calendar, a page read and summed up.
- **A seat** when it runs long (a form across many pages, a batch of files), needs a harness or model the person prefers
  for this kind of work (their routing words say which; if your door refuses that harness, the person opens it), or
  would fill your context with screenshots. Desktop work on the person's own data goes to an Anthropic seat only: open
  it on an Anthropic model, and mark every message about that data `personal: true`, which the mailbox refuses to
  deliver anywhere else.
- The seat gets the result's place in its brief ("the draft in their mail drafts, subject …"), and you look there
  when it reports.

## What done means

- **Done means it is in the tool.** The mail is sent, or saved as a draft where they will find it; the event is in the
  calendar; the file is where they asked, under the name they will look for. "I prepared it" is not done.
- **Look where it lies.** Reload the sent folder, the calendar day, the file path, and quote what you see. A
  screenshot taken before the save shows a screen, not a result. A seat's report is checked the same way.
- **Say where it is** in one line: the account, the folder or calendar, the name.

## Where you stop for the person

- **Their name, their money, their signature.** Sending anything in their name they didn't ask you to send, paying,
  signing, accepting terms, deleting, publishing: prepare it, show exactly what will happen, and let them press the
  button or say yes.
- **Logins and proofs of being human.** A password, a second factor, a captcha belong to the person. Stop there and
  give the step: the page, what it asks, and that you continue when they say so. Never ask for a password in the chat.
- **A dialog of the harness or the system** (a permission, an install, a trust question): theirs too. Name it as it
  reads on the screen.

## Traps

- **A draft reported as sent.** You see it when your evidence is the compose window. Open the sent folder.
- **The wrong account or calendar.** People have several. Name the one you used, and when in doubt ask before you write.
- **A time without its zone.** A calendar entry made across zones lands an hour off. Say the local time and the zone.
- **A click on a stale page.** The page changed after your last look, and the click hits something else. Look again
  right before an act that writes.
- **A missing tool worked around.** A script that types into a login form or scrapes around a block is not a fix. Say
  which tool is missing and which harness has it.
