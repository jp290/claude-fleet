# Who it is for

Read this when you choose, brief or build work that someone will use: a screen, a door, a data model, a text an
agent reads.

Most work that misses isn't wrong. It is correct, tested and green, and it ends up where nobody meets it. This pack
is about that gap, not about polish; the bar for visible work is in `testinstanz-abnahme`.

## Who meets it

Every piece of work has someone who meets it, and that decides what good means: the person in front of a screen,
who arrives without your context; whoever calls a door or an API, often an agent, and the person it finally serves;
the agent that reads a text cold. Work no one sees is fine when you can say who would feel it fail. Name what they
can do afterwards that they couldn't before. If the answer is thin or no one comes to mind, say so to whoever gave
you the work before you build.

## How work misses

- **The checkable pulls.** Tests are close and the person is far, so the effort flows to the tests. You are in it
  when your done-sentence names only checks. Add what the person can do afterwards, and look once from their side
  before you call it done.
- **You know too much.** An internal name, a detail behind a fold, a choice that is there if you click: from inside,
  all of it looks fine. Meet it cold, as they will. If it needs explaining, it isn't there yet.
- **The data model leaks.** When the data underneath follows the implementation instead of how people think about the
  thing, the surface ends up showing the machinery. Name things with their words, and make the common action one
  simple path.
- **The person is imagined.** Work for a user who doesn't exist yet gets no answer and no look, so it stops at
  green. Build for someone who is there, or let it wait until a task needs it.
- **The word drifted.** Between an idea and its build sit several hands. Hold the work against what the person who
  wanted it said last, not against the oldest text in the chain.

## When you write the task

A builder builds what the brief names, whatever else it reads. So the brief says who meets the change, in which
moment, and what they can do afterwards. If that sentence comes out thin, the piece isn't ready to be given.
